# Verification and proof engineering

## Verify the exported claim

A certificate is complete only when its exported theorem has the exact target type and all required hypotheses have been discharged. A conditional theorem can use only standard axioms while assuming the estimate still missing from the research.

Writing a score into metadata, checking a helper lemma, or loading a cached module does not prove the target.

## The completion checklist

1. **Contract:** theorem parameters match the score and radius metadata.
2. **Sources:** every coefficient count, rank bound, derivative guarantee, and characteristic gate uses the selected parameters.
3. **Counting:** all component types and exceptional strata are covered, including shared factors and leading-coefficient zeros.
4. **Domain:** split, phase, and threshold receipts cover the full required degree range.
5. **Error budget:** the mutual-correlated-agreement (MCA) and list contributions satisfy the same certified target.
6. **Policy:** imports, source paths, file counts, and byte totals satisfy the pinned verifier configuration.
7. **Build:** changed prerequisites are rebuilt; stale compiled files cannot supply the result.
8. **Kernel:** audit the theorem's assumptions and perform the required full kernel verification.

A replacement interpolation source can change a degree budget even when it restores positive kernel dimension. The changed budget must propagate through every dependent counting receipt.

## Make proof obligations small

Prefer a shared mathematical reduction followed by small exact receipts. Useful techniques include closed forms for affine sums, structural natural-number recursion, and a proved implication from a cheap checker to the full predicate.

An interval or corner reduction needs a theorem covering the omitted points. Sampling is not an equivalent certificate. A large table should not be checked through one enormous definition if it can be represented by smaller independent obligations.

Avoid unnecessary unfolding of large arithmetic expressions. Targeted rewrites and preserving parameter projections can reduce kernel conversion work. Measure the relevant proof phase rather than attributing import-time variation to the tactic.

## Interpret timings correctly

A native leaf check with cached imports measures that leaf. A separate replay of its declarations is stronger evidence about those declarations, but is not a fresh verification of the entire submission closure.

Use the verifier's concurrency and resource settings when measuring a complete candidate. Bound process lifetimes so timed-out diagnostics do not leave child processes consuming resources. Keep research logs and PDFs outside the submission payload.

The two meanings of proof size must remain separate: Lean source/checking cost and transmitted cryptographic proof bytes. Reducing one does not automatically reduce the other.
